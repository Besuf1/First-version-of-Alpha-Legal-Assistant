# English — Main institutions and sources

The Ethiopian Intellectual Property Authority (EIPA) administers intellectual-property services. Key sources include Trademark Registration and Protection Proclamation No. 501/2006 and Regulation No. 273/2012; Copyright and Neighboring Rights Protection Proclamation No. 410/2004 as amended by Proclamation No. 872/2014 and registration regulation; and Inventions, Minor Inventions and Industrial Designs Proclamation No. 123/1995 and Regulation No. 12/1997.

A company name, trade name, domain name, copyright, patent, and trademark are different rights. Registering a company or trade name does not by itself register a trademark. EIPA expressly warns that trade-name registration alone does not provide trademark protection.

# English — Trademarks

A trademark is a visible sign capable of distinguishing one person's goods or services from those of another. It may include words, designs, letters, numerals, colors, shapes of goods or packaging, or combinations. See Trademark Proclamation Article 2 and EIPA's trademark guidance.

Ownership rights binding on third parties are acquired through a trademark registration certificate. A registrable mark must be capable of distinguishing goods or services and must not fall within refusal grounds, including conflict with earlier third-party rights, deception or confusion, non-distinctiveness, prohibited descriptive or customary signs, public-order or morality issues, sound, or smell. See Articles 4–7.

An application covers one mark and identifies the relevant classes of goods or services. A foreign applicant must appoint an agent domiciled in Ethiopia. See Article 9. EIPA recommends a clearance search before filing or use.

Registration lasts seven years from the application date and may be renewed for consecutive seven-year periods. See Articles 24–25. Non-use for a continuous period of at least three years before a cancellation request may create cancellation risk, subject to the statutory conditions and force-majeure exception. See Article 35.

# አማርኛ — የንግድ ምልክት

የንግድ ምልክት የአንድን ሰው ዕቃ ወይም አገልግሎት ከሌላው ለመለየት የሚችል የሚታይ ምልክት ነው። ቃላት፣ ንድፎች፣ ፊደሎች፣ ቁጥሮች፣ ቀለሞች፣ የዕቃ ወይም የማሸጊያ ቅርጽ ወይም ጥምረት ሊሆን ይችላል።

በሦስተኛ ወገኖች ላይ ተፈጻሚ የሆነ ባለቤትነት በምዝገባ ምስክር ወረቀት ይገኛል። ምልክቱ ልዩነት ማሳየት የሚችል መሆን አለበት፤ ከቀድሞ መብት ጋር የሚጋጭ፣ የሚያሳስት፣ መለየት የማይችል፣ በተለምዶ የሚጠቀሙበት፣ ድምፅ ወይም ሽታ የሆነ ምልክት ሊመዘገብ አይችልም። አንቀጽ 4–7ን ይመልከቱ።

የንግድ ምልክት ምዝገባ ከማመልከቻ ቀን ጀምሮ ለ7 ዓመት ይጸናል፣ በተከታታይ ለ7 ዓመት ጊዜያትም ሊታደስ ይችላል። አንቀጽ 24–25ን ይመልከቱ። ቢያንስ ለ3 ተከታታይ ዓመታት ያለበቂ ምክንያት አለመጠቀም ለስረዛ ሊያጋልጥ ይችላል።

# English — Copyright

Copyright protection arises without formality when the statutory requirements are met; registration is not a precondition to protection. EIPA offers voluntary registration, and its certificate can serve as prima facie evidence of ownership. See Copyright Proclamation Article 6 and EIPA's registration guidance.

Protected works can include literary, artistic, musical, audiovisual, photographic, architectural, software, sound-recording, and broadcasting works, subject to the law's definitions, originality or form requirements, exclusions, ownership, and limitations.

Under the general rule in Article 20, economic rights belong to the author during life and to heirs or legatees for fifty years after death. Different periods apply to some categories, including photographic and broadcasting works. Moral rights and ownership or commissioned-work questions require separate analysis.

Economic rights may be assigned or licensed in whole or part. Copyright or software commissioned from an employee, founder, contractor, designer, or developer should be addressed clearly in a written agreement; the chatbot must not assume that payment automatically transfers all rights.

በአጠቃላይ የኢኮኖሚ መብት በደራሲው ሕይወት ዘመንና ከሞቱ በኋላ ለ50 ዓመት ለወራሾች ይቆያል። ለአንዳንድ የሥራ ዓይነቶች ልዩ ጊዜ አለ። አንቀጽ 20ን ይመልከቱ።

# English — Patents and utility models

EIPA states that a patent protects an invention that is new, involves an inventive step, and is industrially applicable. The patent initially lasts fifteen years from the Ethiopian filing date, may be extended for a further five years where the statutory working requirement is proven, and requires annual renewal fees. See Proclamation No. 123/1995 and EIPA's patent guidance.

A utility-model certificate is granted for five years and may be renewed for a further five years when the statutory conditions are met. Public disclosure before filing can destroy novelty. An inventor should obtain patent advice before publishing, pitching without protection, selling, demonstrating, or uploading technical details.

A patent does not automatically give regulatory permission to make or sell a product and does not override another person's rights or sector law.

# አማርኛ — ፓተንትና ጥቃቅን ፈጠራ

EIPA እንደሚገልጸው ፓተንት አዲስ፣ የፈጠራ ደረጃ ያለውና በኢንዱስትሪ ተግባራዊ ሊሆን የሚችል ፈጠራን ይጠብቃል። ፓተንት መጀመሪያ ከኢትዮጵያ ማመልከቻ ቀን ጀምሮ ለ15 ዓመት ይጸናል፣ በሕጉ መሠረት ለተጨማሪ 5 ዓመት ሊራዘም ይችላል፣ ዓመታዊ ክፍያም ያስፈልጋል።

የጥቃቅን ፈጠራ ምስክር ወረቀት ለ5 ዓመት ይሰጣል፣ መስፈርቱ ከተሟላ ለተጨማሪ 5 ዓመት ሊታደስ ይችላል። ከማመልከት በፊት ፈጠራውን ለሕዝብ ማሳየት አዲስነቱን ሊያጠፋ ይችላል።

# English — Startup IP checklist

1. List trademarks, software, designs, inventions, content, data, domain names, and trade secrets.
2. Identify the actual creator, employer, founder, contractor, funder, and owner.
3. Obtain written assignments or licenses where needed.
4. Search before adopting a brand and file in the correct classes.
5. Assess patentability before public disclosure.
6. Use NDAs and practical access controls for confidential know-how.
7. Put IP ownership, permitted use, open-source software, warranties, infringement responsibility, and exit rights in founder, employment, contractor, investment, and customer agreements.
8. Calendar filing, opposition, renewal, use, annual fee, and evidence-preservation dates.
